# The Best Resume Template for Executive and C-Suite Roles

## Executive Resumes Are Narratives, Not Lists

At the VP, SVP, and CXO level, the resume stops being a credentialing document and becomes a story. Recruiters and search firms are no longer asking "can this person do the job?" — they assume yes — they're asking "what's the through-line of this leader's career, and does it set them up to deliver what we need next?"

That shift changes everything about the format. Bullet count drops. Whitespace expands. Each role gets a paragraph, not a list. Numbers move from the granular (lines of code, tickets closed) to the consequential (revenue grown, businesses turned around, organizations scaled).

The Trusted Taupe template is built for this register. It's spacious, serif, and quiet — designed to hold a senior story without competing with it.

## Why Spacious Serif Layouts Build Trust

Three design choices in Trusted Taupe combine to communicate seniority:

**Serif typography.** Serif fonts are the typographic default of formal documents — annual reports, legal opinions, board materials. Using one signals familiarity with that world.

**Spacious density.** A senior resume that's crammed onto one page reads as anxious. Spacious density gives each role room to breathe, which lets the achievements register one at a time rather than blurring together.

**Divider headings, not boxes.** Section headings in Trusted Taupe are separated by a thin horizontal rule rather than a colored bar. This is the visual language of editorial design and McKinsey decks — restrained, confident, and unmistakably senior.

The color palette is a warm taupe and stone — not gray (which can feel cold) and not navy (which can feel corporate). It's the visual register of a Harvard Business Review essay, not a recruiter's stock template.

## Trusted Taupe: A One-Column, Divider-Heading Format

Trusted Taupe uses a single column intentionally. At the executive level, the work history is the resume — it should not be elbowed by a sidebar. A skills sidebar reads as junior at this level; you don't list "leadership" the way an analyst lists "SQL."

The layout opens with a hero block (name, current title, contact buttons, executive summary) and then runs sections vertically: Work Experience, Education, Awards, Board Service, Certifications. There's room for a video resume in the hero if you have one — increasingly common for board candidates and PE-backed CEO searches.

Density is set to spacious, with section title size at large. The page should feel composed, not packed.

## Leading With a Career Summary, Not a Headline

Below the hero, an executive resume opens with a 4–6 line career summary. Not a headline ("Strategic CEO") and not an objective ("Seeking a role where I can…"), but a paragraph that names what you do, who you do it for, and what you've delivered.

A strong example: "Operating CEO with 18 years scaling B2B SaaS companies from $20M to $200M ARR. Two PE-backed exits (3.4x and 5.1x MOIC) and one strategic sale to a Fortune 100 acquirer. Recognized for turning product-led businesses into enterprise-led ones without losing the developer-first DNA."

Three sentences. Specific. Quantified where it counts. Lets the recruiter form a thesis before reading the work history.

A weak example to avoid: "Visionary leader with a passion for driving results." That sentence applies to everyone and tells the recruiter nothing.

## How Long Should an Executive Resume Be?

Two pages is the right length for almost every executive resume. One page is too short to do justice to a 20-year career; three pages reads as undisciplined.

Use the second page for early-career roles (one or two lines each), board service, advisory work, speaking engagements, and education. The first page should hold the current role, the previous role, and the role before that — each with a paragraph of context and 3–5 high-impact bullets.

Academic CVs are an exception (they can run 8–20 pages with publications and grants), but for corporate executive searches, two pages is the convention. Trusted Taupe's spacious density makes two pages feel like the natural length, not a stretch.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is two pages OK for an executive resume?

Yes, two pages is the standard at VP level and above. Recruiters and retained search firms expect it. One page is too short for a senior career; three pages reads as undisciplined. Trusted Taupe is laid out so two pages feel natural — the spacious density and one-column flow give a senior story room without padding.

### Should I include early-career roles?

Yes, but compress them. List your first three or four roles in a single "Earlier Experience" block at the bottom of page two: title, company, dates, one line of context. This lets recruiters trace your trajectory without giving disproportionate weight to a 25-year-old job. Hide a role completely only if it's irrelevant or actively distracting.

### Do executives still need a skills section?

Mostly no. At the executive level, your skills are inferred from your roles and accomplishments, not listed. The exception is technical executives (CTO, Chief Data Officer, CISO) who may want a brief technologies block to clarify domain. For everyone else — CEOs, CFOs, COOs, CHROs, CMOs — drop the skills section and let the work history carry the signal.
