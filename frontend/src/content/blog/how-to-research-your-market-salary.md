# How to Research Your Market Salary and Negotiate with Confidence

## Why Salary Research Matters More Than You Think

According to a 2025 PayScale study, 57% of workers have never negotiated their salary, and those who do negotiate earn an average of $5,000–$10,000 more per year. Over a 30-year career, that gap compounds to $300,000–$600,000 in lost earnings.

The biggest barrier to negotiation isn't fear — it's uncertainty. When you don't know your market rate, you can't negotiate from a position of knowledge. Salary research transforms a subjective conversation ("I think I deserve more") into an objective one ("The market rate for this role in this city at my experience level is $X–$Y, and here's the data").

This guide shows you how to find your number, understand what it means, and use it in a real negotiation.

## Understanding Salary Percentile Bands

Salary data is typically presented in percentile bands:

- **P10 (10th percentile):** The bottom 10% of earners. Usually entry-level or below-market.
- **P25 (25th percentile):** Below average. Typical for early-career professionals or lower cost-of-living areas.
- **P50 (median):** The midpoint. Half earn more, half earn less. This is the "typical" market rate.
- **P75 (75th percentile):** Above average. Senior professionals, high-demand markets, or strong negotiators.
- **P90 (90th percentile):** Top of market. Staff/principal level, FAANG companies, or expensive metros.

## How to Find Your Market Rate (Step-by-Step)

1. **Use the Builderforce Salary Calculator.** Enter your role, seniority, city and work mode. It returns a P25–P75 band and lists every multiplier it applied to get there, so you can check the assumptions rather than take the number on faith.
2. **Cross-reference with 2–3 other sources.** Glassdoor, Levels.fyi (for tech), the Bureau of Labor Statistics, and LinkedIn Salary Insights all provide useful data points. No single source is perfect.
3. **Adjust for total compensation.** Base salary is one component. Factor in bonuses (typically 10–20% for senior roles), equity/RSUs, benefits value, and perks. A $150K base with $50K in RSUs is worth $200K total comp.
4. **Account for cost of living.** $130K in Austin has more purchasing power than $160K in San Francisco. Use a cost-of-living calculator to normalize.
5. **Target the P50–P75 range.** This gives you room to negotiate up while staying within market bounds. Asking above P90 will usually end the conversation.

## How to Negotiate Your Salary

**Timing.** Never share a number first. When asked "What are your salary expectations?", respond with "I'd like to understand the full scope of the role before discussing compensation. What's the budgeted range for this position?" In many states, employers are now legally required to disclose salary ranges.

**Anchoring.** When you do share a number, anchor at P75. If the market P50 is $140K and P75 is $160K, say "Based on my research and experience level, I'm targeting $155,000–$165,000." This gives the employer room to meet you in the middle while keeping you above median.

**The counteroffer.** If their initial offer is below your target, don't accept or reject immediately. Say: "Thank you for the offer. I'm excited about the role. Based on market data for [role] in [city] at [X years experience], the typical range is [P50]–[P75]. Given my [specific value add], I'd like to discuss a base of [your target]." Then go silent.

**Beyond base salary.** If the employer can't move on base, negotiate: signing bonus, equity, remote flexibility, PTO, professional development budget, or title. These have real value and are often easier for companies to approve.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How accurate is the Builderforce Salary Calculator?

The Salary Calculator uses aggregated market data combined with AI estimation to produce P10–P90 ranges. It's most accurate for common roles in major US markets. For niche roles or non-US locations, cross-reference with 1–2 other sources like Glassdoor or Levels.fyi.

### Should I negotiate salary for my first job?

Yes, even for entry-level roles. Research shows that not negotiating your first salary costs an average of $500,000 in lifetime earnings due to compounding raises. Even a $3,000 increase in your starting salary adds up significantly over time.

### What if the employer asks for my current salary?

In many US states (including California, New York, Colorado, and Washington), it's illegal for employers to ask for salary history. Even where it's legal, you can redirect: 'I'd prefer to focus on what's fair for this role based on market data. What's the budgeted range?'
