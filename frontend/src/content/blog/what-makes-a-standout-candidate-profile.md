# What Makes a Standout Candidate Profile (And How to Build One)

## Recruiters Scan Profiles in 7 Seconds — Here's What They Read

Recruiter behavior on profile pages mirrors recruiter behavior on resumes: a 5–10 second skim that decides whether to keep reading or move on. The profiles that survive that skim all pass it for the same reasons.

In the first scan, a recruiter is looking for four things: the **headline** (does this person describe themselves the way the role would describe the candidate?), the **specialty** (do they have a clear lane?), the **metrics** (is there at least one quantified outcome to anchor on?), and the **signal of activity** (open to work, recent profile updates, follower / connection counts).

Most candidate profiles fail the skim because they're generic — "experienced professional with a passion for results" — or because they bury the specialty under a corporate title that means nothing outside the company.

## The Headline Is the Whole Battle

Look at the headline on any profile that gets replies. Notice the structure:

- "Senior Product Designer • Shipped onboarding from 38% → 61%"
- "Staff Software Engineer • Distributed systems, payments, Go/Rust"
- "Enterprise AE • 142% to plan FY24, $4.8M ARR closed"

Each has the same shape: **role + bullet of differentiation**. The role anchors the recruiter to the lane ("yes, this person fits the kind of search I'm running"), and the differentiator answers the next question ("why this person specifically?").

Weak headlines do the opposite: "Passionate about helping companies grow," "Driven leader with a track record of success," "Results-oriented professional." These say nothing because they apply to everyone.

Rule of thumb: if your headline could swap onto any other person's profile in your industry without anyone noticing, it's wrong. Add one specific number, technology, or outcome that only applies to you.

## Why Quantified Differentiators Beat Adjectives

Notice that all three sample headlines use numbers, technologies, or named outcomes — never adjectives.

"Shipped onboarding from 38% → 61%" tells a recruiter exactly what kind of designer Jane is (one who works on growth surfaces, measures results, and ships material lifts). "Senior Product Designer with a focus on user experience" tells them nothing.

"Distributed systems, payments, Go/Rust" gives a recruiter the three signals that decide whether Marcus's profile matches their open req in seconds. "Senior software engineer with a passion for clean code" doesn't.

"142% to plan FY24, $4.8M ARR closed" is a sales recruiter's dream — it pre-qualifies Priya before they've read another word. "Driven sales professional with a record of success" forces them to dig.

If you can't think of a specific number, look at your last role and find one — deal size, team size, revenue impact, system scale, project count. Even a small number beats an adjective.

## The Activity Signals That Push You Up the List

Beyond the headline, a strong profile carries quieter signals that affect search ranking and recruiter trust:

**Open to work badge.** Profiles flagged as open to work surface higher in recruiter searches. If you're actively job-seeking, turn this on. If you're passively open, turn it on with the "only recruiters" visibility setting.

**Recent activity (lastActiveAt).** Profiles that haven't been touched in 6+ months read as stale. Recruiters quietly skip them. Even a small update — adding a new skill, refreshing a headline, changing the location — refreshes the activity timestamp.

**Connection / follower counts.** Not the most important signal, but they do communicate "this is a real, established professional" rather than a half-finished profile. Don't game this — but don't neglect it either.

**Avatar.** A real photo — or a tasteful illustrated avatar — outperforms an empty silhouette by a wide margin in click-through rates from recruiter search.

## How to Audit Your Own Profile in 5 Minutes

Pull up your profile and run these five checks — or skip the manual pass and let [Profile Audit](/tools/profile-audit) grade them for you and propose a rewrite of your headline and summary you can accept in one click.

1. **Headline test.** Read it out loud. Could you swap your headline with three peers' headlines and not notice the difference? If yes, rewrite it with one specific number, technology, or outcome.

2. **Skim test.** Open your profile and time yourself reading it for exactly 7 seconds. What did you absorb? If you didn't catch your specialty and at least one quantified achievement, your profile fails the recruiter skim.

3. **Activity test.** When was your last update? If it's been over 90 days, make a small change — new skill, updated headline, refreshed summary — to bump your activity timestamp.

4. **Open-to-work test.** Are your work preferences set correctly? Recruiters filter heavily on this; mis-setting it can mean zero inbound for months.

5. **Avatar test.** Real photo or thoughtful illustrated avatar? If you're using a default silhouette, fix it now. The single highest-leverage 30-second change you can make.

Most candidate profiles can move from "forgettable" to "high-skim" with 15 minutes of focused editing. The checklist above is the target.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I use a real photo or an illustrated avatar?

A real, professional photo wins for most use cases — it humanizes the profile and increases click-through. Illustrated avatars work fine when used consistently and intentionally, but a default silhouette is always worse than a thoughtful illustrated avatar, and a thoughtful illustrated avatar is always worse than a real photo.

### How long should my profile headline be?

60–80 characters is the sweet spot. Long enough to fit a role and a differentiator, short enough that the entire headline reads in one glance. The sample headlines above are all in this range. Treat the headline like a tweet — every word has to earn its place.

### How often should I update my profile?

Touch the profile every 30–60 days, even if the change is small. Recruiter search algorithms quietly down-rank stale profiles, and active candidates surface higher in inbound. A new bullet, an updated headline, or a refreshed summary is enough — you don't need to do a full rewrite.
