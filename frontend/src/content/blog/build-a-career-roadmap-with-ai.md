# How to Build a Career Roadmap with AI: Targets, Gaps, and a 90-Day Plan

## Why Most Career Goals Never Happen

"I want to be a senior product manager" is a wish, not a plan. It has no target you can measure against, no list of what's actually missing, and no dated steps. So nothing moves — you stay busy without getting closer.

A career roadmap fixes all three. It starts with a *specific* target role, runs an honest gap analysis between where you are and where that role expects you to be, and then sequences the gaps into dated milestones. The difference between a wish and a roadmap is that a roadmap tells you what to do *this week*.

The hard part has always been the gap analysis. You can't see your own profile the way a recruiter does, and you don't know which of the dozen things you could improve actually move the needle for the role you want. That's the problem an AI career planner solves: it reads your profile the way a recruiter would, scores it against the target, and tells you which gaps matter most.

## Step 1 — Pick a Target You Can Actually Hit

Every roadmap starts with a destination. In [Career 360](/tools/career-360), the AI reads your profile and resume and proposes three grounded targets:

- **A next step** — a believable move in the next 6–12 months.
- **A stretch** — a more ambitious role on a 12–24 month horizon.
- **A pivot** — a lateral move into an adjacent field, grounded in skills you already have.

Nothing is invented. Every suggestion ties back to a signal already on your profile, so you're not chasing a role you have no path into. If none fit, skip the suggestions and type your own — any title, seniority, and timeline. The same engine runs against whatever you enter.

The rule for a good target: it should feel slightly uncomfortable but not delusional. A target you're already qualified for produces an empty roadmap; a target three levels up produces a roadmap you'll abandon.

## Step 2 — Run the Gap Analysis (The Eight Signals)

Once you've set a target, Career 360 scores your readiness across the eight signals recruiters actually weigh when they evaluate a candidate:

1. **Resume** — is it tailored, quantified, and ATS-readable for this role?
2. **Profile** — is your public profile complete and aligned with the target?
3. **Skills** — do you have (and visibly demonstrate) the skills the role requires?
4. **Experience** — does your history show the scope and seniority expected?
5. **Certifications** — do you hold the credentials that gate this role?
6. **Narrative** — does your summary tell a coherent story toward this target?
7. **Outreach** — are you visible to and in contact with the right people?
8. **Salary** — is your compensation expectation calibrated to the market for this role?

Each signal gets a score, and the wheel makes the gaps obvious at a glance — a short spoke is a gap, a full spoke is a strength. This is the part you can't do for yourself: it's an outside read of your candidacy, scored against the specific role you're aiming for rather than in the abstract.

## Step 3 — Turn Gaps into a 30/60/90/180-Day Plan

A gap you can see is still just a gap. The roadmap is what makes it actionable. Career 360 sequences your gaps into four horizons:

- **30 days** — the quick wins. Usually resume and profile fixes: tailor your resume to the target, fill out the missing profile sections, rewrite your summary so the narrative points at the role you want. These move your scores fast.
- **60 days** — the skill and credential work that takes a few weeks. Start a certification, build a portfolio piece, close a visible skills gap.
- **90 days** — the outreach and positioning work. Start conversations with people in the target role, get visible in the right places, line up references.
- **180 days** — the deeper experience gaps. The stretch assignment, the side project, the scope you need to demonstrate before the target is realistic.

The sequencing matters: quick wins first builds momentum and your scores climb early, which keeps you in the plan. Tackling the hardest experience gap on day one is how roadmaps die.

## Step 4 — Close Gaps and Watch the Scores Move

Each gap on your roadmap links to the specific tool that closes it — Resume Optimizer for the resume gap, Profile Audit for the profile gap, Skill Extractor for skills, and so on. You don't have to figure out *how* to close a gap; the roadmap hands you the tool.

When you run a linked tool, your scores update. Career 360 plots every recorded run as a sparkline under the wheel — a running record of improvement over time. That feedback loop is what separates a roadmap from a to-do list: you can see the spoke that was short three weeks ago filling in, which tells you the work is paying off and keeps you moving to the next gap.

Re-run the target analysis periodically. As you close gaps, your profile changes, and a target that was a stretch in month one may become a believable next step by month three — at which point you set a new, higher target and the roadmap regenerates.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a career roadmap?

A career roadmap is a dated plan that connects where you are now to a specific target role. It has three parts: a measurable target, a gap analysis showing what's missing between you and that role, and a sequence of dated milestones (typically 30/60/90/180 days) for closing those gaps. Career 360 generates all three from your profile.

### How does the AI decide what my gaps are?

Career 360 reads your profile and resume and scores eight signals recruiters weigh — resume, profile, skills, experience, certifications, narrative, outreach, and salary — against the target role you picked. A low score on a signal is a gap; the roadmap then sequences those gaps into dated milestones, quick wins first.

### Can I set my own target role instead of using the suggestions?

Yes. The AI proposes three grounded targets (a next step, a stretch, and a pivot), but you can skip them and type any title, seniority, and timeline. The same gap analysis and roadmap engine runs against whatever target you enter.
