# How to Book a Career Coaching Session on Builderforce

## When Coaching Is Worth It

A coaching session is worth it when you're stuck on something specific and a second set of expert eyes would unstick you faster than another week of guessing. The highest-ROI uses: a resume that isn't getting callbacks, interview prep before a round that matters, a career pivot you can't see the path through, or salary-negotiation strategy on a live offer.

Coaching is targeted, not generic. The best sessions start with a concrete problem — "I have a final round on Thursday and I keep stumbling on the systems-design portion" — rather than "help me with my career."

## Step 1: Find a Coach and Book a Slot

Open [Bookings](/meetings) to see coaches and their available session types and times. Pick the session that matches your need — resume review, mock interview, career strategy — and choose an open slot. Booking reserves the time and confirms the session.

When you book, write a sentence or two about what you want from the session. A coach who knows your goal before the call walks in prepared, and you spend the session on the work instead of on context-setting.

## Step 2: Prepare So the Session Pays Off

A coaching session is short; preparation is what makes it count. Before the call: have your materials ready (resume, job description, the offer), write down your two or three most important questions, and decide what a successful session looks like to you.

For a resume review, Builderforce also has a dedicated resume-feedback workflow where a reviewer can leave inline annotations — sticky notes, highlights, and comments — and even propose suggested edits as a draft, all kept separate from your resume so they never print. Pairing a live coaching session with written annotations gives you both the conversation and a durable record to act on afterward.

## Step 3: One-Off vs Ongoing Coaching

A single session is right for a specific, bounded problem — review this resume, prep for this interview. Ongoing coaching is right for a longer arc: a career change, a leadership-skills push, a months-long job search where you want consistent accountability.

Start with one session. If it delivers and you have a longer journey ahead, ask the coach about a recurring arrangement. The first session also tells you whether this is the right coach for you — fit matters as much as credentials, and one session is a low-cost way to find out.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where do I book a coaching session?

On the Bookings page, where you can see coaches, their session types, and available times. Pick the session that matches your need — resume review, mock interview, or career strategy — choose an open slot, and add a note about your goal so the coach arrives prepared.

### Can I get written resume feedback as well as a live session?

Yes. Builderforce has a dedicated resume-feedback workflow where a reviewer leaves inline annotations — sticky notes, highlights, and comments — and can propose suggested edits as a draft. Those annotations are kept separate from your resume so they never print, giving you a durable record to act on alongside the live conversation.

### Should I book one session or ongoing coaching?

Start with one. A single session suits a specific problem like reviewing a resume or prepping for an interview. If you're facing a longer arc — a career change or a months-long search — ask about ongoing coaching after the first session, which also lets you confirm the coach is the right fit.
