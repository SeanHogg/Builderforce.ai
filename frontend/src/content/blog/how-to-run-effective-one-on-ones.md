# How to Run Effective 1:1s (and Actually Track the Follow-Through)

## The 1:1 Is the Employee's Meeting, Not Yours

The most common 1:1 failure mode is a manager using the time as a status update — a verbal stand-up that the chat tool already covers. A great 1:1 is the *employee's* meeting: their agenda, their concerns, their growth. Your job is to listen, unblock, and coach, not to collect a progress report.

Reframing the meeting this way changes everything. When the report owns the agenda, the conversation drifts toward the things that actually matter — blockers, career direction, friction with the team — which are exactly the signals you'd otherwise learn about too late.

## Cadence: Weekly, 30 Minutes, Protected

For most reports, weekly is the right default — frequent enough to catch problems while they're small. Thirty minutes is usually plenty if it's focused. The non-negotiable is that you *protect* the time: cancelling 1:1s when you're busy sends the message that the person is optional, and that's precisely when they most need the time.

If weekly is genuinely too much for a senior, autonomous report, biweekly can work — but never skip silently. A reliably-kept 1:1 is one of the strongest trust signals a manager sends.

## A Simple, Durable Agenda

You don't need an elaborate template. A durable structure: (1) how are you doing — genuinely, not the reflexive "fine"; (2) what's blocking you that I can help with; (3) progress and priorities; (4) growth and career; (5) feedback, both directions.

Keep a shared, running agenda the report can add to between meetings. On Builderforce's [1:1s](/ceremonies), the agenda and talking points live in one place both people can see, so the conversation builds on the last one instead of starting cold every week.

## Capture Action Items — or the 1:1 Leaks

The fastest way to erode trust is to agree to something in a 1:1 and then forget it. Every 1:1 should end with explicit action items and an owner for each. On Builderforce, those action items are tracked as tasks tied to the conversation, so they carry forward and you can open the next 1:1 by closing the loop on the last one.

"Last week you raised X and I said I'd do Y — here's where that landed" is the single highest-trust sentence a manager can open with. It only works if the system remembered, which is why capturing items beats relying on memory.

## What to Listen For

Beyond the agenda, 1:1s are your early-warning system. Listen for the quiet signals: a normally engaged person going flat, repeated frustration with the same blocker, a report who's stopped talking about growth. These show up in 1:1s weeks before they show up in performance — if you're listening for them.

The 1:1 is where retention is won or lost. People rarely leave over a single event; they leave over a slow accumulation of unaddressed friction. The manager who surfaces and acts on that friction in the 1:1 is the manager whose team stays.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should I hold 1:1s?

Weekly for most reports, 30 minutes, and protect the time — don't cancel when you're busy, since that's often when the report most needs it. Biweekly can work for senior, autonomous reports, but never skip silently.

### Whose meeting is the 1:1?

The employee's. The most common failure is a manager turning it into a status update. Let the report own the agenda and use your time to listen, unblock, and coach — that's what surfaces the blockers and career concerns you'd otherwise learn about too late.

### How do I keep 1:1 commitments from being forgotten?

Capture explicit action items with an owner at the end of every 1:1 and track them. On Builderforce, 1:1 action items are tracked as tasks tied to the conversation, so they carry forward and you can open the next meeting by closing the loop on the last one.
