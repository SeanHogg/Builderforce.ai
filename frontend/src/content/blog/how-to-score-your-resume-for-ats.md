# How to Score Your Resume for ATS Compatibility in 2026

## What Is an ATS Resume Score?

An ATS (Applicant Tracking System) resume score is a numeric rating that measures how well your resume matches a job posting. Companies like Oracle (Taleo), Workday, Greenhouse, and Lever use ATS software to filter candidates before a human ever reads your resume. Studies show that up to 75% of resumes are rejected by ATS before reaching a recruiter.

Your ATS score typically evaluates four dimensions: keyword match (do your skills mirror the job description?), formatting compatibility (can the parser extract your sections?), content quality (are achievements quantified?), and completeness (contact info, dates, education). A score above 80% generally means your resume will pass the initial filter.

## Why Your Resume Keeps Getting Rejected

The most common reasons resumes fail ATS screening are surprisingly simple to fix:

**1. Missing keywords.** If the job says "project management" and your resume says "PM", the ATS may not match them. Always mirror the exact phrasing from the job description.

**2. Bad formatting.** Tables, text boxes, headers/footers, and multi-column layouts break most ATS parsers. Stick to a single-column layout with standard section headings (Experience, Education, Skills).

**3. Wrong file type.** Some ATS systems struggle with PDFs that were exported from design tools like Canva. Use a .docx or a text-based PDF from Word or Google Docs.

**4. No quantified achievements.** "Managed a team" scores lower than "Managed a team of 12 engineers, delivering 3 releases per quarter." Numbers signal impact.

## 10 Tips to Improve Your ATS Score

1. **Use standard section headings.** "Work Experience" beats "Where I've Made Impact."
2. **Mirror job description keywords** word-for-word in your skills and bullet points.
3. **Quantify everything.** Revenue, percentages, team sizes, timelines.
4. **Use a single-column layout.** No tables, no text boxes, no graphics.
5. **Include a skills section.** List hard skills explicitly — don't assume the ATS infers them from context.
6. **Spell out acronyms once.** Write "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)" the first time.
7. **Use standard fonts.** Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman — avoid custom or decorative fonts.
8. **Save as .docx or text-based PDF.** Avoid image-heavy PDFs.
9. **Don't stuff keywords.** ATS systems (and recruiters) penalize unnatural repetition.
10. **Tailor for each job.** A generic resume scores 20–30% lower than a tailored one on average.

## What's a Good ATS Score?

The scoring bands break down like this:

- **90–100% (A+/A):** Excellent. Your resume will pass virtually any ATS filter. Only 8% of resumes score here on the first try.
- **80–89% (B+/B):** Strong. You'll pass most filters but may miss highly competitive roles. A few keyword tweaks can push you into the A range.
- **70–79% (C+/C):** Average. You'll pass some filters but get rejected by strict ones. Focus on keyword matching and quantifying achievements.
- **Below 70% (D/F):** Needs work. Likely getting auto-rejected. Check formatting, add missing skills, and tailor to specific jobs.
