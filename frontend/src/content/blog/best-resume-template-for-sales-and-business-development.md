## Sales Hiring Is Numbers Hiring

Of every job category, sales is the most numbers-driven. A sales recruiter is hunting for one specific data point in the first 10 seconds: did you hit your number? If yes, by how much? Across how many quarters?

Most templates make this hard to find. Sales achievements get buried inside paragraph-length work bullets, mixed with activities and territory descriptions. The Growth Emerald template fixes this by putting a confident, ALL-CAPS "Career Highlights" treatment at the top of the work history — each role led by quota attainment, ARR closed, or quota-to-plan figures rather than activities.

The palette is intentional too. Green is the universal color of growth and revenue, used confidently but not garishly. Recruiters at sales-heavy companies (HubSpot, Salesforce, Snowflake, etc.) recognize the signal immediately.

## Growth Emerald: A One-Column, Achievement-Forward Layout

Growth Emerald uses a single-column print layout with comfortable density and ALL-CAPS section headings. One column matters here because sales recruiters scan top-to-bottom — a sidebar would split their attention away from the achievement narrative.

The color treatment is restrained: emerald accents on section headings, role names, and key metrics; black-on-cream for the body. Quota attainment numbers can be optionally bolded for skim-reading.

Density stays comfortable rather than compact because sales bullets need room — a strong sales bullet is two lines minimum (deal size, complexity, what you did, the outcome).

## How to Write Quota-Led Bullets

Replace activity-led bullets with achievement-led ones. The structure is consistent across role levels.

**Lead with the quota result (1 line).** "142% to plan FY24 ($4.8M closed against $3.4M target); top 5 of 28 AEs."

**Describe the territory or motion (1 line).** "Territory: West Coast Series A–C SaaS, ICP 50–500 employees. Motion: ABM-led outbound with 3-touch sequencing."

**Highlight one or two signature deals (1–2 lines).** "Closed $1.2M expansion at [public company name] — largest deal in segment in FY24, displaced incumbent vendor in 4-month cycle."

**Mention pipeline / activity stats only if exceptional (optional 1 line).** "Generated 38% of personal pipeline through self-sourced outbound."

This format compresses what a sales recruiter actually wants to know into 4–5 lines per role. Compare that to a paragraph about "managing accounts and growing relationships" — invisible.

## How to Frame Multiple Quarters and Plans

Sales numbers tell a story when you show consistency over time. A single year at 142% looks like a hot streak; three years at 110%+ looks like a pattern.

Use a small "Career Highlights" block at the top of the resume (or in the summary line) to surface multi-quarter consistency:

**Career Highlights:** 142% / 118% / 127% to plan (FY24/23/22) · $11.4M cumulative ARR closed · 3x President's Club · Top 10% of segment all four quarters of FY24

This block is what a recruiter screenshots and forwards to the hiring manager. It tells the whole story before they read a single role.

For SDRs and BDRs, the equivalent metrics are meetings booked, qualified pipeline generated, and conversion rates — not quota dollars. Use the same callout structure with the metrics that match your function.

## Mistakes to Avoid

**Don't say you "managed a territory."** Every AE manages a territory. Quantify it: territory size, ICP, average deal size, sales cycle length.

**Don't use percentages without dollars.** "127% to plan" without a plan size is meaningless. "127% to plan ($3.2M target)" is hireable.

**Don't list every account.** Five named, recognizable logos beat twenty unknown company names. Pick the most strategic.

**Don't pad with sales-skill keywords.** "MEDDIC, BANT, Challenger Sale, SPIN selling" — listing methodologies is fine, but only if you can speak to applying them in interviews. Don't list a methodology you can't articulate.

**Don't skip activity metrics for SDRs/BDRs.** At the top of funnel, activity volume + conversion rates are the resume. "82 dials/day average; 38% connect-to-meeting conversion" tells a hiring manager you understand the math.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I include my W-2 / total comp on the resume?

Generally no. Comp belongs in the recruiter conversation, not on the public resume. The exception is roles where comp progression itself signals something — "Promoted from SDR to AE within 9 months; OTE grew from $80K to $180K" can work in a tight summary line. Avoid listing actual W-2 numbers on the resume itself.

### How do I handle a year where I missed quota?

Don't hide it; frame it. If FY23 was 87% to plan but FY22 and FY24 were 120%+, list all three together so the average tells the story. If you had a structural reason for a miss (territory change, market downturn, leadership turnover), one short line of context is fine: "FY23 87% — territory restructured mid-year; recovered to 127% in FY24."

### Is green too aggressive for enterprise sales?

Growth Emerald is intentionally restrained — emerald is used only for section headings and accent metrics, with the body in black-on-cream. It reads as confident but professional, suitable for enterprise sales through VP Sales roles. For very conservative enterprise contexts (banking, government sales), Iron Gray or Trusted Taupe may be a safer call.
