# The Best Resume Template for New Grads and Interns (2026)

## Why New Grads Need a Different Template

Most resume templates are built for people who already have a career to describe. They put work experience first, then education at the bottom. That order makes sense for someone with five jobs and a ten-year-old degree.

For a new grad, it's backwards. Your degree is your strongest credential; your one summer internship is your weakest. Putting work first forces recruiters to scroll past your thin work history before reaching the part of your background they actually care about.

The Education First template inverts the order. Education leads. Projects and coursework get prominent placement. Work experience — when you have any — comes after, framed as supporting evidence rather than the main argument.

## Education First: Putting Your Strongest Asset on Top

Education First uses a balanced blue palette, sans-serif font, and underlined headings — a clean, modern look that reads as student-appropriate without feeling juvenile.

The section order is deliberate: Education → Work Experience → Projects → Skills → Volunteer → Awards → Languages → Interests. Education at the top means the recruiter sees your school, degree, GPA, and graduation date in the first three seconds. For new-grad hiring, those four data points often determine whether you make the next round.

For each education entry, include: institution, degree, major (and minor if relevant), graduation date, GPA (if 3.5+), and 3–5 lines of detail — relevant coursework, honors, study abroad, leadership roles. Don't be modest here. Coursework is your work history at this stage.

## How to Use Projects and Coursework as Experience

Projects are how new grads compete with candidates who have internship experience. A well-described capstone, hackathon win, or independent project can carry more weight than a generic intern summer.

For each project, write three lines:

**1. What it was.** "Built a real-time transit dashboard for the campus shuttle system."

**2. What you did specifically.** "Designed the React frontend, integrated the GTFS-RT feed, and deployed to Vercel."

**3. The outcome.** "Adopted by 1,200 students in the first month; demoed at the university CS showcase."

The outcome line matters most. "Built a thing" is generic; "1,200 users adopted it" is hireable. Even small numbers (100 users, 5 GitHub stars, won 3rd place) are better than no numbers.

## Quantifying Work Without "Real" Work

If your only work history is a campus job, a tutoring gig, or a part-time service role, frame it with the same rigor you'd use for a corporate role. Recruiters at competitive new-grad programs (consulting, banking, big tech) don't penalize part-time work — they penalize fuzzy descriptions.

Good: "Tutored 14 introductory CS students weekly; raised section average grade from C+ to B over one semester."

Weak: "Helped students learn computer science."

Good: "Managed inventory across 6 SKU categories at a 2,400-sq-ft retail store; reduced stockouts by 22% by switching to a weekly reorder cycle."

Weak: "Worked at a retail store."

The quantification habit is the most transferable skill from a part-time job to a full-time role. Build it now.

## When to Switch to a Work-First Template

The Education First template is right for the first 0–18 months after graduation. After that, your work experience starts carrying the resume and the order should flip.

Specific signs you've outgrown Education First:

- You have one full-time role and 1+ years of post-graduation experience.
- You've been promoted or had your scope materially expanded.
- You have quantified work outcomes that are stronger than your GPA or coursework.
- A recruiter has told you the resume reads as junior.

When you switch, you can re-render the same content into another template (Iron Gray for finance, Asphalt for consulting, the default Builderforce for general use). Your content stays; only the order and layout change.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I include my GPA?

Include your GPA if it's 3.5 or above and you're within five years of graduation. Below 3.5, leave it off — recruiters who care will ask, and listing a low GPA is worse than not listing one. After five years, work experience eclipses GPA regardless of the number.

### Is one page mandatory for new grads?

Yes, with rare exceptions (PhD candidates, candidates with substantial published research). A two-page new-grad resume usually means padding. Education First is designed to fit a strong new-grad story comfortably onto one page when you trim early-life roles and lengthy paragraph descriptions.

### Should I list relevant coursework?

Yes — but pick carefully. List 4–6 courses that match the role you're applying to, not your entire transcript. For a software role: Algorithms, Distributed Systems, Databases, Machine Learning. For a finance role: Corporate Finance, Financial Modeling, Econometrics. Generic intro courses (Intro to Psychology) signal nothing and crowd the page.
