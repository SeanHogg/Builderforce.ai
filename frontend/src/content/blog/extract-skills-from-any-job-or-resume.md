# How to Extract Every Skill from a Job Description or Resume with AI

## Why Manual Skill-Matching Fails

When you read a job description by hand, you miss things. A single posting can name 25–40 distinct skills scattered across the summary, the responsibilities, the requirements, and the "nice to haves" — and your eye glazes over half of them. Worse, you can't reliably tell which are deal-breakers and which are wish-list items.

Keyword search doesn't save you either. It misses synonyms ("k8s" and "Kubernetes" are the same skill), conflates skills with company or product names, and can't distinguish a hard skill from a soft one. So you end up either over-claiming skills you don't have or, more often, under-selling because you didn't realize a requirement mapped to something already on your resume.

An AI skill extractor reads the source the way a recruiter does: it pulls every skill mentioned, collapses synonyms, drops the noise, and groups what's left so you can actually reason about it.

## Turn Extraction into a Skills-Gap Comparison

Extracting one source is useful; comparing two is where it pays off. Extract the skills from a job you want, then extract the skills from your own resume, and the gap is immediate: the skills the job names that your resume doesn't is your to-do list.

That gap drives two actions. First, **add the skills you genuinely have but forgot to list** — these are free wins that instantly raise your match. Second, **identify the real gaps** — the skills you'd need to learn or demonstrate before you're a credible candidate, which feed directly into a development plan.
