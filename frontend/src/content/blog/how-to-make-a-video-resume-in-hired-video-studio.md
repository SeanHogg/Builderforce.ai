# How to Make a Video Resume in the Builderforce Creation Canvas (60-Second Method)

## Why Studio Beats Recording on Your Phone

You can record a video resume on your phone, but you'll fight three things: staying on script, fixing the one scene where you stumbled, and getting clean audio. the Builderforce Creation Canvas solves all three in the browser — a teleprompter that scrolls your script, scene-by-scene retakes so one bad take doesn't cost you the whole video, and audio leveling on export.

The whole loop — script, record, edit, render, publish — happens in one tab. Render runs client-side with ffmpeg.wasm, so there's no upload-and-wait.

## Step 1: Script It in Scenes

Open [Studio](/creation-canvas), create a video document, and write your script broken into scenes. The proven 60-second shape is: hook (one specific claim), name and role, one story (problem, action, result), and a close that names the role you want.

Keep each scene to one idea. Scenes are the unit you'll re-record, so a tight scene break means a clean retake. Aim for 60–90 seconds total — 130–180 spoken words.

## Step 2: Record with the Teleprompter

Turn on the teleprompter and it scrolls your script over the camera preview while you record, so you keep eye contact instead of reading off to the side. Record scene by scene. If you fumble scene 3, re-record just scene 3 — the rest stays untouched.

Look at the lens, not your own image. Sit one step more formal than the company's day-to-day. Record each scene a few times and keep the take that sounds conversational, not memorized.

## Step 3: Polish — Voiceover, Trims, Music

If you'd rather not be on camera for part of the video, generate AI voiceover from your script for those scenes. Trim dead air at the head and tail of each scene. Add a quiet music bed if it fits your tone, set its level under your voice, and export handles the leveling.

Don't over-edit. A video resume that's visibly cut every two seconds reads as anxious. Two or three clean scene transitions beat a dozen jump cuts.

## Step 4: Render and Publish

Export renders the final MP4 in your browser. Publish it to your profile as your video resume, attach it to applications, or grab the public `/v/<id>` link to share anywhere.

Update it when your headline metric goes stale or your role changes — for most people that's once every 12–18 months. Re-recording one scene is cheap, so keeping it current is easy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should a video resume be?

60–90 seconds. Longer videos don't get watched to the end; shorter ones don't have room for a story. Front-load your strongest, most specific claim in the first three seconds so the recruiter decides to keep watching.

### Can I re-record just one part?

Yes. Studio records scene by scene, so you can re-record a single scene without redoing the whole video. That's the biggest advantage over recording one continuous take on your phone.

### Do I have to upload my footage to render?

No. Studio renders the final video client-side with ffmpeg.wasm, so your raw footage doesn't need to be uploaded to a server to produce the finished MP4.
