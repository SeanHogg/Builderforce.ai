# How to Leave a Company Review That Actually Helps People

## Why Your Review Matters More Than You Think

Company reviews are how the next candidate decides whether to accept an offer, negotiate, or walk away. A specific, honest review can save someone from a bad fit or steer someone great toward a hidden gem. A lazy one — five stars, no detail, or a one-star revenge post — just adds noise.

Builderforce reviews are bound to authenticated accounts, one per user per company, and rated across six sub-axes rather than a single star. That structure exists so reviews stay useful and hard to game. Your job as a reviewer is to fill that structure with honest specifics.

## Rate All Six Axes Honestly

Open the company on [Companies](/companies) and leave a review rated across the six axes: culture, leadership, work-life balance, compensation, career growth, and diversity & inclusion. Resist the urge to make them all match. Most real workplaces are uneven — great culture with brutal hours, or strong pay with no growth path.

The *shape* of your ratings is the most useful thing you give a future candidate. A company that's a 5 on culture and a 2 on work-life balance tells a far more honest story than a flat 3.5 across the board. Rate each axis on its own merits.

## Write Pros, Cons, and Advice That Are Specific

Specificity is everything. "Good culture" helps no one; "weekly cross-team demos meant I always knew what other teams were building" helps a lot. For each of pros, cons, and advice to management, give concrete examples a reader can picture.

Keep it about patterns, not people. "My manager was bad" is a grievance; "managers here have wide spans and limited time for coaching" is a structural insight a candidate can use. The most valuable reviews describe how the place actually works, not a single bad day.

## Earn the Verified-Employee Badge

A review carries far more weight when readers know it's from a real employee. On Builderforce you can earn a verified-employee badge by matching your corporate email domain at review time. Verified reviews are the ones candidates trust most.

Verification also protects the system: because reviews are account-bound and sub-axis ratings are stored as auditable numeric values, the platform can detect suspicious bursts and drift. Leaving a genuine, verified, specific review is the single most useful thing you can do for the next person weighing an offer from your company.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What makes a company review actually useful?

Specificity and honest unevenness. Rate all six sub-axes on their own merits rather than making them match, and back each rating with concrete pros, cons, and advice a reader can picture. The shape of your ratings and real examples help far more than a flat star count.

### How do I get my review to carry more weight?

Earn the verified-employee badge by matching your corporate email domain at review time. Verified reviews are the ones candidates trust most, and because reviews are account-bound and sub-axis ratings are auditable, the system can flag suspicious activity.

### Should I write about my specific manager?

Focus on patterns over people. 'My manager was bad' is a grievance; 'managers here have wide spans and little time for coaching' is a structural insight a candidate can act on. Describe how the place works, not a single bad day.
