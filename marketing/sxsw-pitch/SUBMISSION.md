# SXSW Pitch — BuilderForce.ai submission brief

Working answers for the [SXSW Pitch](https://sxsw.com/pitch/) entry form. This is
the source content; the live, scored version of it lives on a Creation Canvas
built from the **Pitch competition war room** pack, where the entry, the pitch,
the scorecard, and the Q&A drill are each objects that grade themselves against
the competition's own rules.

## The six criteria, and what we answer with

**Innovation / originality.** Agent platforms orchestrate models. BuilderForce
ships Evermind — a self-updating SSM whose law is write-through cognition, where
an update *replaces* rather than accumulates — so the system that does the work
also learns from it. The delivery loop and the model are the same product, not an
integration between two.

**Viability.** Bring-your-own-keys means adopters keep the frontier models they
already pay for; BuilderForce is additive rather than rip-and-replace, which
removes the largest objection in the category. Tenant-billed gateway plus
self-host means cost to serve scales with usage rather than ahead of it.

**Marketability.** The buyer is the engineering or delivery leader who already
owns a backlog and a model spend. The motion starts in the tools they are in —
VS Code, the board, the repo — and the same canvas is the surface a
non-engineering stakeholder can use, which is what widens the seat count.

**Potential for growth.** Every pillar compounds on the same graph: projects,
tickets, agents, diagnostics, canvas objects. Adding a capability adds value to
the ones already there rather than starting a new adoption curve.

**Capacity for impact.** The measured claim, not the aspirational one: we
instrument the autonomous delivery loop end to end and publish what fraction of
tickets actually reach Done without a human. Naming the real number — and the
human-gated review stage that produces it — is more credible in a Q&A than a
round one.

**Team / people.** Founder-operator with a shipped track record across delivery
tooling; the résumé is the evidence, and the values are visible in the product's
own governance surface (policy packs, human-in-the-loop approvals, audit log).

## Three-minute run sheet

| Beat | Time | What it has to land |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0:20 | One sentence a judge repeats to another judge an hour later |
| Problem | 0:25 | Who is hurting and what it costs them |
| Solution | 0:35 | What we built, in the customer's words |
| Demo | 0:35 | Brief → working system, live |
| Traction | 0:25 | Real numbers with dates |
| Market & model | 0:15 | Who pays, how much, how big |
| Team | 0:15 | Why these people |
| Ask | 0:10 | The single thing we want from the room |

Total 3:00. The script under it must also *speak* in three minutes — roughly 390
words at a measured pace. The canvas checks both.

## Q&A the judges will actually ask

Rehearse the ones we would rather not be asked first:

- What stops a frontier lab from shipping this next quarter?
- What is your real autonomous completion rate, not your best case?
- Why is a self-updating model safer than retrieval over a fixed one?
- Who has paid you, and what did they stop paying for instead?
- What breaks first at ten times the current load?

## Open before submitting

The numbers in "Traction" and "Funding raised to date" must be filled from
current figures at the time of entry, and the eligibility boxes confirmed against
the cap table. They are deliberately not written here — a stale number in a
submission is worse than a blank one.
